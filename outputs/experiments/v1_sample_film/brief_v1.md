## The story

In a highland village in the 1980s, every September, when the hills turn yellow with adey abeba (the Meskel daisy), a grandmother climbs the hill at dawn to gather flowers for the Meskel festival. She always keeps one flower apart: it is for her son, who left for Addis Ababa and rarely comes home. She presses that flower between the pages of her old Bible, and on the night of the Demera bonfire she prays for him. Years later, the family opens the Bible and finds thirty pressed flowers: one for every September he was away.
