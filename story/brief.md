# The Comet of Dessie

**The author's own childhood memory**, written by the author on 26 November 2026. It replaces the fictional sample of version 1 (kept in `outputs/experiments/v1_sample_film/`).

My grandmother used to say that Dessie began with a light in the sky. An emperor was camped beneath Mount Tossa, she told me, and one night he looked up and saw a comet burning over the mountain. He was so happy that he gave the place its name: Dessie, my joy.

I was seven when I decided to find a comet of my own. I built it myself: two old socks, a fistful of plastic bags from the market, and a torn sleeve from my uncle's shirt, all wound with string until my fingers ached. It was not round. It was closer to an egg, and it bounced wherever it wanted.

In Dessie, nothing is flat. Our pitch climbed too: a strip of red earth between a eucalyptus fence and a drainage ditch, tilted like a table with one short leg. Two stones made the goal at the top, two schoolbags the goal at the bottom. The laws of our sefer were simple: whoever owns the ball never plays goalkeeper; whoever kicks it into the ditch climbs in after it; we switch sides at half-time, because the team shooting downhill has gravity as a twelfth player; the match ends when a mother calls.

There was Seid, who could dribble past his own shadow; Dawit, who narrated every match like a radio commentator, including his own mistakes; and Hayat, who was not supposed to be playing at all and was better than every one of us. Her left foot was the reason the ditch rule existed.

The match I remember best was 4–4, next goal wins, shooting uphill, when the fog came down from Tossa like someone pulling a white gabi over the whole town. The ball came to me. I couldn't see the stones, or the goalkeeper. I kicked it as hard as I have ever kicked anything. It rose into the fog and vanished. From somewhere inside the white came Dawit's commentator voice: "GOOOOOL! Dessie has scored!" We never found the ball.

When I told my grandmother, she laughed and pulled me under her gabi. "A comet doesn't stay," she said. "It only shows you where your joy is." The next day I started winding a new one.

When people ask me where I'm from, I say Dessie, and then, before they can ask anything else, I tell them what it means.

## For the film

- **Place and time:** Dessie, a town climbing the side of Mount Tossa in Ethiopia's highlands; a childhood neighbourhood (sefer) with a eucalyptus fence, tin roofs, red earth; cold mornings, rainy-season fog.
- **People:** the author at seven; his grandmother; friends Seid, Dawit and Hayat. All are illustrated; no photograph of anyone is used.
- **The object:** a lopsided ball made of socks, plastic bags and string, "the comet".
- **The feeling:** playful joy, then wonder, and a smile about where you come from.
